Enterprise AI architecture.

From intelligent applications to the platforms, memory systems and governed capabilities that make enterprise AI work.

The hard part is rarely getting a model to produce a useful answer. It is letting an AI system work with enterprise data, invoke real business capabilities, keep the right context and operate reliably inside organisational boundaries. This work draws on enterprise AI platform architecture, SAP-connected agents, commercial AI products and two decades of enterprise integration.

Overview

Four layers, five concerns that cut across them

Not every enterprise process needs an agent. Deterministic workflows, APIs and conventional software remain the backbone; agentic reasoning is added where it gives a clear advantage, and calls the same governed capabilities.

Agent orchestration & runtime

Orchestrating agents, tools and business workflows

Enterprise agents need more than a model and a set of tools. They need a runtime that manages execution, coordinates specialist capabilities, preserves context and handles failures or interruptions.

A central design decision is separating the continuity of a user interaction from individual agent executions. A session can hold many runs, and each run can be tracked, interrupted, resumed and evaluated on its own, instead of treating a whole conversation as one execution.

Architecture topics

  • Agent and capability registries
  • Specialist agents and orchestration
  • Session versus execution (run) state
  • Workflow planning and delegation
  • Checkpoints and recovery
  • Human approval and intervention
  • Execution history and observability

Python, LangGraph, enterprise APIs, runtime persistence and checkpoints, containerised services.

Memory & context engineering

Memory that extends beyond the conversation

Temporary conversational context, historical interactions, durable knowledge and operational state serve different purposes and should not be treated as interchangeable.

The design problem is less about persistence than about judgement: what should be remembered, how knowledge is updated, who may access it, and when stored information should stop influencing a decision.

MemoryPurpose
WorkingContext needed for the current execution
SessionContinuity across a conversation or active workflow
EpisodicHistorical interactions, events and outcomes
SemanticDurable facts, relationships and structured knowledge
ProceduralReusable instructions, processes and execution patterns
Long-termInformation persisted and available across sessions

Design considerations

  • User, agent, workspace and enterprise scopes
  • Private versus shared memory
  • Promotion and consolidation
  • Retrieval relevance and temporal validity
  • Provenance and access control
  • Retention and correction
  • Vector and graph-based retrieval

Enterprise capabilities & SAP integration

Connecting AI to enterprise systems

An agent becomes operationally useful when it can work with real business capabilities, not only retrieve information. This combines two decades of SAP and enterprise integration with AI tool architecture: OData services, REST APIs, reusable tools, capability discovery and governed transaction execution.

The challenge is making business capabilities available to AI systems while keeping enterprise authorisation, validation, auditability and operational controls intact. MCP is treated as an interoperability layer in the architecture, alongside OData and REST rather than in place of them.

Architecture topics

  • Capability and tool registries
  • API and tool discovery
  • SAP OData metadata and service interfaces
  • REST-based enterprise integration
  • MCP interoperability
  • Identity and authorisation propagation
  • Transaction validation and approval-controlled execution
  • Error handling and audit trails

In context

SAP Integration Suite, OData, REST, MCP, Event Mesh, Microsoft Graph, S/4HANA and BTP.

Enterprise knowledge & retrieval

Grounding AI in enterprise knowledge

Enterprise information is spread across business systems, documents, databases and processes. The objective is relevant, authorised and traceable context, with a clear line between retrieved evidence, model-generated interpretation and business-system records.

Applied in ESG intelligence, where teams query sustainability data in plain language, and in master data enrichment and classification.

Technical areas

  • Vector search and semantic retrieval
  • Knowledge graphs
  • Hybrid structured and unstructured retrieval
  • Document ingestion and transformation
  • Entity resolution and fuzzy matching
  • Master data enrichment and classification
  • Provenance and relevance

Governance, evaluation & reliability

Engineering AI for operational control

Reliability cannot rest on model accuracy or a policy document alone. Systems that touch operational processes or execute transactions need enforceable permissions, controlled execution, evaluation, monitoring and clear responsibility for decisions and actions.

These concerns are designed in from the start. Accountability stays with people and the organisation: an AI system acts on delegated authority, and someone owns every outcome.

Design considerations

  • Capability-level authorisation
  • Human-in-the-loop approvals
  • Runtime policy enforcement
  • Tool invocation logging
  • Model and workflow evaluation
  • False-positive and false-negative analysis
  • Drift and changing operating conditions
  • Failure handling and recovery
  • Auditability and incident investigation
  • System-level risk from interacting agents
